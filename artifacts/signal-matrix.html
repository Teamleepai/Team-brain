That is the only place where a top-1% sales motion and the implementation blueprint both get paid — and it is a complexity threshold, not a company-size band.

What changes the small-vs-mid question

Two elite closers whose muscle is $200K–$1.9M complex deals cannot be deployed against a $49/month self-serve product — and the usual fix, making it up on volume, is closed off by the domain-protection rule. Low deal value and low outreach volume is a revenue ceiling, not a strategy.

Part two · the correction to “count the boxes”

Two more axes, multiplied — not added

Summing pain signals floats the worst-run, least-solvent businesses to the top: a company with no website, no SEO, an IVR and bad reviews may simply be badly run and broke. Any axis near zero must zero the prospect.

Part three · sourced

The wall isn't location count. It's the arrival of procurement

Mid-market — 100–1,000 employees, $10M–$250M revenue — is where committees form: 5–8 stakeholders across finance, IT, legal and the business unit, and 3–4 months on a $25K–$100K deal. Below that line, half of all buying groups are just 2–4 people.

Target Under ~100 employees, under ~$50M revenue, under ~10 locations — and one owner or one executive decides. No security review, no procurement veto, no committee to out-resource.
Edge of viable 100–150 employees. Expect 4–7 stakeholders and a 3–4 month cycle. Winnable, slowly. Procurement often arrives late and renegotiates price long after the business unit is already sold.
Do not enter PE platform HQ, 20+ locations, any formal vendor-management function. Centralized procurement is a designed value-creation lever in the roll-up thesis — “add 200 basis points of margin from centralized procurement and the IRR thesis closes itself.” That is an institution built to grind vendors, not a big prospect.

The arbitrageSix locations on one Boulevard instance is one integration and one configuration — not six. Deal value scales with patient count; delivery cost does not scale with locations. That is how you get a deal worth a complex sale that Hamza and Javi can still execute.

On looking smaller

Don't compete on parity — in a parity comparison, smaller always loses

Make principals-on-every-call the actual differentiator: “you get the two people who built this, for the life of the contract, not a team you meet once at kickoff.” That only lands on buyers already burned by a large vendor — which makes “previously burned” a targeting signal, visible in review complaints and recent vendor changes.

Challenge to the call

Industry-agnostic discovery, industry-concentrated selling

A startup closes a brand gap with reference density, not by appearing bigger. Scraping wide is right. Selling wide is not — it maximizes the conversations where you have no proof. In one narrow vertical you can name six customers who look exactly like the prospect. That is how five people out-credential two hundred.

One honest caveat

The skill transfers. The air cover does not

Selling $1.9M deals inside a large company is not the same as selling $200K as five people. There, procurement had pre-approved the vendor and the buyer's downside was near zero. Now that risk is yours to carry — another argument for staying below the committee line, where one owner can absorb it personally.

The clustering method

There is no database of independent 3–8 location groups. You build it

Scrape the Maps universe → cluster on identical brand name, shared root domain and shared main phone → count distinct addresses → keep clusters of 3–8 → exclude any brand on the consolidator lists → verify ownership on what remains.

Exclusion is the asset

~30%

of collision revenue sits with consolidators — and 0% of it is winnable

~800 home services companies acquired since 2022. Maintain the brand lists as a file with an owner and a refresh cadence — a stale exclusion list is worse than none, because you'll trust it.

The cost nobody priced

Raw data for ~5,000 entities is under $200. Labour is the real line item

The $50-per-qualified-prospect kill criterion will be breached by manual verification hours, not data fees — and it'll be breached quietly. Watch the collision DRP signal, which is manual by nature and has no database behind it.

Part seven · the finding that inverts the plan

Lead flow is not the constraint. Delivery is

Every earlier estimate assumed two callers at 40 dials/day. The real roster has four people at 175 dials/day — roughly three times the pipeline needed. That reverses the central assumption and changes the right move.

EstimatesSet rates are my estimates from your descriptions, not measurements. Gong's benchmark across 300M+ calls is 4.6% average, 16.7% top quartile. Week 4 replaces all four with measured numbers — and the "3× capacity" conclusion rests entirely on them.

Surplus dial capacity is permission to raise price.

When you can afford to lose more deals, you can quote higher — and the same delivery load produces more revenue. You are not short of conversations. You are short of hours to implement what you sell. Don't run at max capacity: run at a third, aim it higher, and spend the surplus on research depth and delivery.

The cadence

1/wk

One close per week for fourteen weeks

That's the entire plan in one line. It needs 5.6 meetings/week against a capacity of ~18 — 31% utilisation. Even at benchmark close rates you'd produce ~36 customers.

The unblock

Cold calling does not need the legal review

Human-dialed B2B calls are legal without prior consent. Only the SMS and email legs need Gate 1. Dialing starts week 3 regardless of where the attorney sits — the review gates channels, not the plan.
