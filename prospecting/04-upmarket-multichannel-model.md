## 3. The central tension you need to decide

**Going up-market raises ACV but lowers close rate and lengthens the cycle — and with a 30 November
deadline, cycle length is the binding constraint.**

| ACV/mo | Annual | Benchmark close rate | Cycle | Customers for $46.5K | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2,500 | $30K | 20–28% | 30–90d | **19** | Underpriced, high volume |
| **$4,000** | **$48K** | **20–28%** | **30–90d** | **12** | **Sweet spot** |
| $6,000 | $72K | 15–22% | 30–90d+ | 8 | Strong, slower |
| $8,000 | $96K | 15–22% | 60–120d | 6 | **Cycle likely overruns Nov 30** |
| $12,000 | $144K | 12–18% | 90–180d | 4 | **Cannot close in time** |

Win rates fall as deal size rises: $10–50K ACV closes at **20–28%**, $50–100K at **15–22%**, and
$100K+ at **12–18%**, with cycles of 30–90 days for mid-market and 90–180+ for enterprise
[[zenitdata.com](https://zenitdata.com/blog/b2b-saas-win-rate-benchmarks-by-deal-size-stage-and-segment-2026/), [optif.ai](https://optif.ai/learn/questions/sales-cycle-length-benchmark/)].

### My recommendation: **$4,000/mo blended, with the Group tier listed at $4,000–6,000**

- **12 customers, not 19.** 3.2 closes/month across 3.8 months
- Still inside the **20–28%** close band — you don't take the up-market win-rate penalty
- Still a **30–90 day cycle**, so two cohorts fit before 30 November
- Still **below** the mid-market agency floor of $5,000, so you're the value option with more scope

Price the *Group tier* at $4,000–6,000 and let close rate arbitrate (doc `03` §2.4). **$8,000+ is the
right 2027 position, and the wrong bet for a November deadline.**

## 4. Reverse-engineering the ad spend

### 4.1 The unit economics of a paid lead

Using B2B benchmarks, not your $1.21:

| | Conservative | **Base** | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cost per lead | $200 (qualified B2B) | **$125** | $63 (B2B average) |
| Lead → customer | 3% | **5%** | 7% |
| **CAC** | **$6,667** | **$2,500** | **$900** |
| Payback at $4,000/mo | 1.7 months | **0.6 months** | 0.2 months |

Lead-to-customer for **professional services is 4.6% — the highest of any B2B category** — and inbound
leads convert at **5–10%** versus outbound at 1–3%
[[martal.ca](https://martal.ca/conversion-rate-statistics-lb/)]. 5% is defensible for inbound.

**Sub-one-month payback at base case is excellent** and means ads should be funded aggressively — but
only once the offer-specific CPL is proven, which it is not yet.

**Do not try to hit the whole number with ads.** At $48K spend for $48K/mo of MRR you'd be fine on

## 5. The activity model — dials, emails, SMS

At $4,000 ACV, 6 customers from outbound over 3.8 months = **1.6 closes/month from outbound.**

### 5.1 Dials

| Stage | Rate | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Closes from outbound | — | **1.6** |
| Qualified opportunities | 25% close | 6.4 |
| Meetings booked | 60% → opp | 10.7 |
| Live connects | 15% set rate | 71 |
| **Dials** | **20% connect** | **356** |

**356 dials/month ≈ 18/day ≈ 9 per person per day** across two callers. That is very comfortable — and
it's comfortable *because* you went up-market. At $1,000 ACV the same target needs ~1,400 dials/month.

**Going up-market cuts required dial volume by roughly 75%.** That is the single strongest argument for
the higher ticket, and it's why price beats activity every time.

### 5.2 Emails

CAN-SPAM applies, not PEWC — so email can precede consent, with identification and opt-out.

| | |
|---|---|
| Prospects entering cadence/month | ~356 |
| Emails each (sweet spot is 3–4) | 3 |
| **Total emails/month** | **~1,070 ≈ 50/day** |
| Expected replies @ 3.43% | ~37/month |

2026 B2B cold email averages **27.7% open, 3.43% reply**; above 5% puts you ahead of most senders
[[instantly.ai](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)].

**50/day sits far under the 5,000/day Gmail bulk-sender line**, which is permanent once crossed. But a
brand-new domain cannot start at 50/day — it needs a **4–8 week ramp** from ~10/day. **Buy and warm the
domain this week or you will not have a usable sender in September.**

### 5.3 SMS — small volume, disproportionate impact

Per doc `02`, SMS only goes to prospects who gave **written** consent after a live call.

| | |
|---|---|
| Connects/month | 71 |
| Written consent @ 40% of connects | ~28 |
| SMS per consented prospect | 4 |
| **Total SMS/month** | **~113 ≈ 6/day** |

Tiny volume — and the highest-performing channel you have. Opted-in SMS response rates average **~45%
versus ~6% for email**, and **qualifying questions by SMS see reply rates up to 92%**
[[verse.ai](https://verse.ai/blog/sms-response-rates-what-the-data-really-shows/), [sakari.io](https://sakari.io/blog/sms-marketing-conversion-rate-what-actually-drives-results-and-how-to-improve-yours)].

**This is why the consent gate is a feature.** 113 fully-consented messages a month at ~45% response
will outperform thousands of cold texts, and carries none of the TCPA exposure.

### 5.4 The full cadence

Research consistently shows **8–12 touchpoints across multiple channels** to book a meeting; direct
outreach averages **8 touches**, and 2–3 follow-ups generate up to **42% of all replies**
[[syncgtm.com](https://syncgtm.com/blog/how-many-touchpoints-for-a-b2b-sale)].

```
  PRE-CONSENT                          POST-CONSENT (written opt-in captured)
  ─────────────────────────            ──────────────────────────────────────
  Touch 1  Email (research-led)        Touch 5  SMS — AI SDR, seconds latency
  Touch 2  Call  ← the human one       Touch 6  Email — the value case
  Touch 3  Email (follow-up)           Touch 7  SMS — qualifying question (92% reply)
  Touch 4  Call  (second attempt)      Touch 8  Call — book the meeting
                                       + retargeting ads running throughout
```

| Activity | Volume/month | Per day | Constraint check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dials | 356 | 18 | 9/person/day — comfortable |
| Emails | 1,070 | 50 | Far under 5,000/day. Needs domain warmup |
| SMS | 113 | 6 | Consented only. Zero TCPA exposure |
| Ad spend | $3,000–4,000 | ~$120 | Staged behind CPL gates |
| Prospects researched | ~356 | 18 | AI SDR handles this |

## 7. What I'd challenge in your own plan

1. **"Running ads at scale" is aspirational, not current.** $303 spent, everything paused, and the two
   campaigns matching your offer never ran. Treat the ad channel as unvalidated and gate the spend.
2. **Your $1.21 CPL will not survive.** Different offer, instant-form leads, and B2B benchmark is 52x
   higher. Model at $125–200 and be delighted if you beat it.
3. **You're underpriced against your own client size, badly.** A 5-location group pays $3,000–6,000 for
   SEO alone. You're proposing $2,500 for SEO *plus four other products*.
4. **But don't go too far up-market for this deadline.** Above ~$6,000/mo the close rate drops to 15–22%
   and cycles stretch past 90 days. With 3.8 months you get one cohort, not two. $8,000+ is the 2027
   move.
5. **The month you just lost is the real risk.** "Collect" versus "run-rate" moved the build deadline to
   ~1 September. Four weeks. That, not the revenue number, is what will break this.
6. **Referral revenue is missing from your plan entirely.** You have customers. Two referrals at $4,000
   is $8,000/mo — a sixth of the target — for essentially zero CAC. It is the cheapest line in this
   model and nobody has assigned it to anyone.
