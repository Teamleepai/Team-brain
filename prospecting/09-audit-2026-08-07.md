the cost is real and you should hold it consciously:

**Net read: the timing hook is the real loss.** Partially recoverable — med spa has its own year-end
expiry angle (HSA/FSA funds), and HVAC's heating season runs through the sell window. Neither is as
clean as "your benefits expire December 31," but the Q4 urgency story survives.

## 2. Defects found — beyond the scope change

Four real problems, independent of dental and veterinary.

### D1 · Doc `03` is built on superseded assumptions with no warning — **CRITICAL**

`03-deployment-plan-50k-mrr.md` states the deadline as **31 December** with a build freeze at
**1 October**, and models the gap as **$50,000 from zero MRR.**

All three are now wrong. Doc `04` established that "collected" moves the signature deadline to
**~30 November** and the build deadline to **~1 September**, and Aaron supplied current MRR of $3–4K,
making the gap **$46,500.**

Doc `04` says it supersedes "`03` §0 and §1" — but **doc `03` itself says nothing.** Anyone opening it
standalone gets a deadline that is a month wrong and a customer count built on a gap that's $3,500 too
large.

**Fixed:** prominent supersession banner added at the top of doc `03`.

### D2 · The v1 workbook is superseded, unmarked, and sitting next to the current one

`50k-mrr-funnel-model.xlsx` models exit run-rate with current MRR at zero. `50k-collected-multichannel-model.xlsx`
is the current model. **Two similarly-named workbooks in one folder, one silently wrong, is a footgun** —
and a spreadsheet is more dangerous than a document because people open it and start typing.

**Fixed:** moved to `prospecting/archive/` with a README explaining why. Not deleted — it's the record
of the exit-run-rate analysis, and git history shouldn't be the only copy of something someone might
want.

### D3 · Doc `04`'s channel mix contradicts the current model

Doc `04` §4.3 proposes **6 outbound + 4 ads + 2 referral = 12 customers** at $4,000 blended.
Doc `05` §6.2 and the live workbook use the **barbell: 5 Group @ $4,500 + 12 Fast @ $2,000 = 17
customers**, sourced 9 outbound + 5 ads + 3 referral.

Both land near $46,500, so neither is arithmetically wrong — but they're different plans with different
customer counts, and nothing in doc `04` says it was refined. A reader comparing them would reasonably
conclude one of us can't count.

**Fixed:** pointer added to doc `04` §4.3 directing to the barbell as the current composition.

### D4 · Doc `07`'s filename became actively misleading

`07-automotive-dental-veterinary.md` now describes content that is two-thirds removed.

**Fixed:** rewritten as automotive-only and renamed to `07-automotive-icp.md`. The old path will 404 —
noted here deliberately so nobody wastes time looking for it.

## 4. Research I tightened

### 4.1 Auto repair revenue conflict — still unresolved, now handled explicitly

Sources disagree: **$450,000** average US repair shop [[gitnux.org](https://gitnux.org/auto-repair-industry-statistics/)]
versus **$500K–1.2M** for independents [[wickedfile.com](https://www.wickedfile.com/blogs/how-much-does-an-independent-auto-repair-shop-make-in-2026/)].

I could not reconcile these, and the gap decides whether a shop can pay $2,000/mo. Rather than pick one,
**auto repair qualification is now explicitly bay-and-technician-based, not revenue-based**, in both doc
`07` and the artifact. A scraper cannot see revenue; it can see bay count in Maps photos and Street
View, and technician count in review text and site copy.

**This is now the top-ranked vertical, so this qualification rule is load-bearing.** It needs to survive
contact with Phase 3.

### 4.2 Collision facility count — flagged, not resolved

IBISWorld reports **105,000** auto body shop businesses; another source cites **8,000+ facilities**
[[ibisworld.com](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/auto-body-shops/1694/), [worldmetrics.org](https://worldmetrics.org/automotive-collision-repair-industry-statistics/)].
Most likely all-registered-businesses versus larger facilities. **TAM sizing for collision should not be
done off either number until it's reconciled.**

### 4.3 The one claim I'd most want re-verified

**"Missed call revenue losses can exceed $75,000/month"** for auto repair
[[autoadvisorpartners.com](https://autoadvisorpartners.com/auto-repair-shop-statistics)] is now the
headline number for your **top-ranked vertical**, and it's a ceiling ("can exceed"), not a median.

The underlying components are better sourced and more defensible: **25–45 calls/day**, **20–30% missed**,
**85% of voicemail callers never call back**. **Build the arithmetic in front of the prospect from those
three numbers rather than asserting $75,000** — it's more credible, it's specific to their shop, and it
survives a challenge. The $75K figure is a headline, not evidence.
